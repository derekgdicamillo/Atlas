# SECTION 1 — GAP / NEEDS ASSESSMENT

## 1.1 Professional Practice Gap Statement

**The gap (what is, versus what should be):**

Licensed prescribers are initiating GLP-1 receptor agonists at an unprecedented rate, but most are doing so without a structured competency in titration, adverse-effect management, and the functional obesity-medicine framework required to keep patients safe and in treatment. The prevailing pattern is **"prescribe and pray"**: the provider writes a semaglutide or tirzepatide script, the patient loses weight initially, and then — with no titration plan, no nutrition/lean-mass strategy, no side-effect algorithm, and no monitoring cadence — the patient develops intolerable GI symptoms, loses muscle along with fat, plateaus, or discontinues and regains. The medication works; the outcome fails, because nothing was built around the prescription.

| Domain | Current practice (what is) | Desired practice (what should be) |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge** | Prescribers know GLP-1s exist and are effective, but many cannot recite the FDA titration ladders, missed-dose rules, or the MTC/MEN2 boxed-warning screen. | Prescribers can construct and adjust evidence-based titration schedules and screen every candidate against absolute contraindications. |
| **Skill / Competence** | Side effects are managed reactively and inconsistently; GI intolerance frequently ends therapy prematurely. | Prescribers apply a stepwise adverse-effect algorithm (dietary → antiemetic → titration adjustment → hold/evaluate) and preserve lean mass through protein and resistance-training counseling. |
| **Practice / Performance** | Little standardized monitoring, documentation, or patient-retention structure; every patient is reinvented from scratch. | Prescribers run a repeatable system — labs, follow-up cadence, informed consent, prior-auth documentation, and retention touchpoints — that sustains outcomes. |

**Root cause:** Existing GLP-1 CME teaches pharmacology and stops there. No widely available program teaches the operational and functional-medicine framework (nutrition, movement/lean-mass preservation, mental health, and root-cause/functional wellness) that determines whether a patient keeps the weight off. This course closes that specific gap.

## 1.2 Underlying Educational Need (type of gap)

- [x] **Knowledge** — prescribers lack current, structured knowledge of titration, contraindications, and monitoring.
- [x] **Skill/Competence** — prescribers cannot reliably execute side-effect management and lean-mass preservation.
- [x] **Practice/Performance** — prescribers lack the practice systems (documentation, follow-up, retention) that translate a prescription into a durable outcome.

## 1.3 Supporting Evidence (citations)

1. **American Association of Clinical Endocrinology (AACE) / American College of Endocrinology — Comprehensive Clinical Practice Guidelines for Medical Care of Patients with Obesity (Endocrine Practice, 2016; updated guidance 2022–2023).** AACE frames obesity as a chronic, complications-centric disease requiring structured, staged pharmacologic management with defined follow-up and response assessment — a level of structure the "prescribe and pray" pattern does not meet. Supports the knowledge and performance gaps around eligibility, staging, and monitoring.

2. **Obesity Medicine Association (OMA) — Obesity Algorithm (annual clinical practice statement, current 2025–2026 edition).** OMA explicitly positions anti-obesity medication within a four-pillar model (nutrition, physical activity, behavior, medical/pharmacologic management) and emphasizes lean-mass preservation and adverse-effect mitigation during pharmacotherapy. The persistence of prescription-only care in the community demonstrates a competence gap relative to this standard.

3. **American Diabetes Association — Standards of Care in Diabetes (Diabetes Care, 2025; carried into 2026), Section 8: Obesity and Weight Management.** ADA sets expectations for incretin-based therapy including proactive reduction of insulin/sulfonylurea doses to prevent hypoglycemia, structured metabolic monitoring, and response-based adjustment at defined intervals. Community prescribing frequently omits these steps, evidencing the skill and performance gaps.

4. **Supporting trial/withdrawal evidence for the "regain when unsupported" claim:** Rubino D, et al. *Effect of Continued Weekly Subcutaneous Semaglutide vs Placebo on Weight Loss Maintenance (STEP 4).* JAMA 2021 — patients regained roughly two-thirds of lost weight after stopping semaglutide, underscoring that pharmacotherapy without a durable maintenance/behavioral system fails long-term. Wilding JPH, et al. *Weight regain and cardiometabolic effects after withdrawal of semaglutide (STEP 1 extension).* Diabetes Obes Metab 2022.

# SECTION 2 — LEARNING OUTCOMES

## 2.1 Overall Course Outcomes (measurable, Bloom's verbs)

Upon completion of this enduring activity, the learner will be able to:

1. **Explain** the pathophysiology of obesity as a chronic, relapsing neurometabolic disease and the incretin mechanism (GLP-1/GIP) that underlies GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy. *(Modules 0–1)*
2. **Differentiate** the FDA-approved anti-obesity incretin agents (semaglutide, tirzepatide; injectable and oral) by mechanism, indication, efficacy, and adverse-effect profile. *(Module 2)*
3. **Apply** FDA-approved eligibility criteria, absolute-contraindication screening (MTC/MEN2 boxed warning), and a structured pre-treatment evaluation to select appropriate candidates. *(Module 3)*
4. **Construct** evidence-based titration schedules for semaglutide and tirzepatide, including tolerability-guided adjustments, missed-dose rules, and shortage/switching strategies. *(Module 4)*
5. **Implement** a stepwise adverse-effect prevention and management protocol for GI symptoms and **recognize and manage** serious events (pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, hypoglycemia). *(Module 5)*
6. **Design** a laboratory-monitoring, progress-tracking, and documentation program that supports clinical safety, prior authorization, and medicolegal defensibility. *(Module 6)*
7. **Adapt** GLP-1 prescribing for special populations — type 2 diabetes, renal and hepatic impairment/NAFLD-NASH, older adults, and reproductive-age patients (including the tirzepatide oral-contraceptive interaction). *(Module 7)*
8. **Appraise** emerging off-label evidence using an evidence hierarchy, and **communicate** weight-loss confounding to patients and colleagues using CME-safe, non-promissory framing. *(Module 9)*
9. **Develop** the practice systems — informed consent, insurance navigation, team workflows, retention touchpoints, and a named differentiation framework — that convert clinical competence into sustained patient outcomes. *(Modules 8, 10)*

*(9 outcomes — within the 6–10 target range.)*

## 5.13 ANSWER KEY WITH RATIONALES

**Module 00**
- Q1 — **B.** The course's central thesis: the medication works but outcomes fail when nothing is built around the prescription (nutrition, movement, monitoring, mental health, engagement).
- Q2 — **B.** Precision Weight Science, Nourishing Health, Dynamic Movement, Mindful Wellness, Functional Wellness.
- Q3 — **B.** Weight loss includes lean mass; without resistance training up to ~40% of weight lost can be lean mass, dropping to 15–20% with resistance training. Cardio alone doesn't prevent sarcopenia.

**Module 01**
- Q1 — **A.** Sustained loss >10% with lifestyle alone occurs in <5% at 5 years; metabolic adaptation and hormonal changes drive regain (Look AHEAD).
- Q2 — **C.** ~50–70% of postprandial insulin is gut/incretin-mediated (Nauck 1986).
- Q3 — **B.** Rapid DPP-4 degradation; this is why DPP-4-resistant analogs were developed.
- Q4 — **C.** REE remained ~500 kcal/day below predicted 6 years later (Fothergill 2016).

**Module 02**
- Q1 — **D.** Wegovy maintenance/max is 2.4 mg weekly (Ozempic max is 2.0 mg).
- Q2 — **C.** −20.9% mean weight loss with tirzepatide 15 mg in SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff 2022).
- Q3 — **A.** Delayed gastric emptying can reduce oral-contraceptive absorption; barrier method for 4 weeks after initiation and each escalation (Zepbound/Mounjaro PI).
- Q4 — **B.** Semaglutide showed ~20% MACE reduction in SELECT; tirzepatide CVOT ongoing.

**Module 03**
- Q1 — **B.** BMI ≥27 with ≥1 weight-related comorbidity (or ≥30 alone).
- Q2 — **C.** Medullary thyroid carcinoma (C-cell tumor).
- Q3 — **B.** The boxed warning is MTC/MEN2 only; papillary/follicular are not C-cell tumors and are not contraindications.
- Q4 — **C.** Calcitonin assesses C-cells; TSH does not.

**Module 04**
- Q1 — **A.** Mild tolerable nausea is expected; follow the schedule and escalate to 1.7 mg. Extended titration is reserved for moderate–severe symptoms.
