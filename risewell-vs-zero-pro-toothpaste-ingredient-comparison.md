# Hydroxyapatite Toothpaste Teardown: RiseWell vs. Zero Pro

**Prepared for:** Derek & Esther (FNP owners, PV MediSpa)
**Date:** 2026-07-02
**Scope:** Full-panel ingredient comparison — RiseWell Mineral (original), RiseWell PRO, and Something Nice "Zero Pro" (TheBentist / Dr. Ben Winters). Verdict on the $45 vs ~$16 pricing gap.

---

## TL;DR

- **RiseWell PRO** is the only product here that publishes its hydroxyapatite architecture honestly: **5% nano + 10% micro HA (15% total)**, full INCI list, and stated RDA. It's the technically superior formula at roughly **1/3 the price.**
- **Zero Pro** is **10% nano-HA** wrapped in influencer marketing. Its headline differentiator — the trademarked **"BioFuse Oil Matrix"** — is just **flaxseed (linseed) oil + rosehip seed oil.** Two cheap botanical oils with no toothpaste-specific clinical backing.
- **Zero Pro does not publish an RDA value.** RiseWell does (PRO ~65, Original ~85).
- At **$45 / 4 oz**, Zero Pro is priced ~2.8x RiseWell PRO with a *weaker* HA package and a marketing-driven "matrix" that adds cost, not proven efficacy. **The formula does not justify the price.**

## 1. RiseWell Mineral Toothpaste (Original)

**Hydroxyapatite:** ~10% **micro-hydroxyapatite** (naturally mine-sourced, France). No nano fraction.
**Published RDA:** ~85 (moderate — this is NOT an ultra-low-abrasive paste; worth knowing for sensitive/exposed-root patients).
**Price:** ~$12–16 / tube.

**Full ingredient list (verbatim, in order):**
> Silica, Sorbitol, Glycerin, Xylitol, Hydroxyapatite, Calcium Carbonate, Propanediol, Potassium Cocoate, Stevia Rebaudiana Extract, Mentha Arvensis (Wild Mint) Oil, Mentha Piperita (Peppermint) Oil, Cinnamomum Cassia (Cinnamon) Bark Extract, Citrus Aurantium Dulcis (Orange) Peel Oil, Citrus Limon (Lemon) Peel Oil, Melaleuca Alternifolia (Tea Tree) Oil, Cellulose Gum, Sodium Gluconate, Menthol, Thymus Vulgaris (Thyme) Extract, Erythritol, Xanthan Gum, Eucalyptus Globulus Extract, Illicium Verum (Anise) Extract.

**Functional breakdown:**
| Role | Ingredient(s) |
|---|---|
| Active remineralizer | Hydroxyapatite (micro, ~10%) |
| Secondary abrasive/buffer | Silica, Calcium Carbonate |
| Humectants | Sorbitol, Glycerin, Propanediol |
| Sweeteners/anticaries | Xylitol, Erythritol, Stevia extract |
| Surfactant (foaming) | Potassium Cocoate (soap-based, SLS-free) |
| Essential oils (antimicrobial/flavor) | Wild mint, peppermint, cinnamon bark, orange, lemon, tea tree, thyme, eucalyptus, anise, menthol |
| Thickeners | Cellulose Gum, Xanthan Gum |
| Chelator/stabilizer | Sodium Gluconate |
| Preservative | None synthetic — relies on essential oils + low water activity |

## 2. RiseWell PRO Mineral Toothpaste

**Hydroxyapatite:** **15% total** = **5% nano-HA** (NanoXIM CarePaste, Fluidinova) **+ 10% micro-HA** (French mine). First mass-market paste to combine both particle sizes — nano penetrates tubules/microdefects, micro fills larger surface lesions.
**Published RDA:** ~65 (lower than the Original — the higher HA load displaces some silica).
**Price:** ~$16 / tube.
**Excludes:** fluoride, SLS, parabens. Marketed safe-to-swallow.

**Full ingredient list (verbatim, in order):**
> Water, Sorbitol, Hydrated Silica, Glycerin, Xylitol, Hydroxyapatite, Calcium Carbonate, Propanediol, Nano-Hydroxyapatite, Potassium Cocoate, Stevia Rebaudiana Extract, Mentha Arvensis (Wild Mint) Oil, Mentha Piperita (Peppermint) Oil, Cassia (Cinnamon) Bark Extract, Citrus Aurantium Dulcis (Orange) Peel Oil, Citrus Limon (Lemon) Peel Oil, Melaleuca Alternifolia (Tea Tree) Oil, Cellulose Gum, Sodium Gluconate, Thymus Vulgaris (Thyme) Extract, Xanthan Gum, Erythritol.

**Note:** INCI lists "Hydroxyapatite" (micro) and "Nano-Hydroxyapatite" as separate entries — transparent particle-size disclosure, which is rare. Surfactant is again Potassium Cocoate (soap), no SLS. No synthetic preservative.

## 3. Something Nice — Zero Pro Toothpaste (TheBentist)

**Hydroxyapatite:** **10% nano-hydroxyapatite (nHAp).** No micro fraction disclosed. (Their marketing claims "3x the nHAp of other brands" — misleading, since RiseWell PRO carries 15% *total* HA.)
**Published RDA:** **Not disclosed.** Silica-based, so likely low-to-moderate, but unverifiable.
**Price:** **$45 / 4 oz** (direct). Same-size Amazon listings/kits discount it, but $45 is the brand's anchor price.
**Excludes:** fluoride, SLS, titanium dioxide, parabens.
**Formulator:** Dr. Ben Winters ("The Bentist"), orthodontist / TikTok influencer.

**Full ingredient list (verbatim, in order):**
> Water (Aqua), Glycerin, Hydrated Silica, Hydroxyapatite (nHAp), Xylitol, Sorbitol, BioFuse™ Oil Matrix [Linum Usitatissimum (Linseed) Seed Oil, Rosa Canina (Rosehip) Seed Oil], Stevia Rebaudiana Extract, Sodium Benzoate, Mentha Piperita (Peppermint) Oil, Xanthan Gum, Potassium Sorbate, Mentha Arvensis (Wild Mint) Oil, Sodium Cocoyl Glutamate, Ultramarines (CI 77007), Zinc Citrate.

**Functional breakdown:**
| Role | Ingredient(s) |
|---|---|
| Active remineralizer | Hydroxyapatite (nano, 10%) |
| Abrasive | Hydrated Silica |
| Humectants | Glycerin, Sorbitol |
| Sweeteners/anticaries | Xylitol, Stevia extract |
| "BioFuse Oil Matrix" | **Linseed (flax) seed oil + rosehip seed oil** — that's the entire proprietary blend |
| Surfactant | Sodium Cocoyl Glutamate (mild amino-acid surfactant) |
| Preservatives | Sodium Benzoate + Potassium Sorbate (synthetic — needed because it's more water-heavy) |
| Antibacterial adjunct | Zinc Citrate (legit — reduces plaque/VSCs, real evidence) |
| Colorant | Ultramarines (CI 77007) — cosmetic blue pigment, purely aesthetic |
| Flavor | Peppermint + wild mint oils |

## Red Flags & Claims Audit (Zero Pro)

- **"BioFuse™ Oil Matrix" = flax oil + rosehip oil.** The trademark and "advanced botanical technology / disrupts harmful bacteria while sparing the good microbiome" language dresses up two commodity seed oils. **No peer-reviewed evidence** supports linseed or rosehip oil in a dentifrice for microbiome-selective antibacterial action at these (unstated, low, bottom-of-list) concentrations. This is a marketing construct, not a validated actives system.
- **Undisclosed concentrations.** BioFuse sits *below* Stevia and preservatives in the INCI order — meaning it's present in trace amounts. The "matrix" is likely <1% of the formula.
- **No RDA published.** For a product sold at premium price on an enamel-repair claim, omitting abrasivity data is a transparency gap.
- **"3x nano-HA" claim** compares only the nano fraction and ignores that RiseWell PRO's total HA (15%) exceeds Zero Pro's 10%.
- **Zinc Citrate is the one genuinely useful adjunct** in Zero Pro — plaque/halitosis control has real backing. But it's a cheap, common ingredient, not a $45 justification.

## Clinical-backing reality check (both brands)

- **Hydroxyapatite for remineralization/sensitivity:** Solid evidence base. Multiple RCTs show 10% microcrystalline/nano-HA is non-inferior to fluoride for caries prevention and superior for sensitivity. **Both products clear this bar** at ≥10%. RiseWell PRO's dual nano+micro at 15% is the most defensible formulation of the three.
- **Xylitol:** Anticaries evidence is real but dose-dependent (therapeutic effect wants ~6–10 g/day across multiple exposures — a toothpaste alone won't deliver that). Present in all three, fine as an adjunct.
- **Essential oils (RiseWell) / seed oils (Zero Pro):** Flavor + mild antimicrobial at best. No concentration-specific clinical claim holds up for either. RiseWell simply doesn't build its price around them; Zero Pro does.

## Verdict

**Zero Pro's formula does not justify $45/tube versus RiseWell PRO at ~$16.**

On the metric that actually matters — the remineralizing active — **RiseWell PRO delivers more (15% dual nano+micro HA vs. 10% nano-only), discloses its particle architecture, and publishes an RDA.** Zero Pro leads with a 10% nano-HA number and then leans on a trademarked "BioFuse Oil Matrix" that is, on inspection, two inexpensive seed oils present in trace amounts with zero dentifrice-specific clinical evidence. Its one legitimate extra (zinc citrate) is a commodity ingredient worth pennies.

You're paying ~$29 of brand/influencer premium for a *weaker* HA package and a marketing story. The formula is competent and clean (mild amino-acid surfactant, no fluoride/SLS/TiO₂), but nothing in it is proprietary or clinically superior enough to command 2.8x the price of a better-formulated competitor.

**Recommendation for the clinic:** If you want a hydroxyapatite paste to stock, use, or recommend to patients, **RiseWell PRO is the smarter pick** — better active load, honest labeling, lower RDA, one-third the cost. If a patient is fixated on Zero Pro because of TheBentist, the fair framing is: "same core active class, less of it, no RDA disclosed, and you're paying for the brand." The only patient-specific reason to reach for Zero Pro over RiseWell would be a desire for zinc-citrate plaque/halitosis control or sensitivity to RiseWell's soap-based surfactant/essential-oil load — and even then, cheaper zinc pastes exist.
